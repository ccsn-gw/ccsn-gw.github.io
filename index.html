The bounce signal and what it encodes

Rapid rotation flattens the collapsing core. At bounce it rings, and the peak frequency and amplitude of that burst depend on rotation (T/|W|) and on the stiffness of nuclear matter. That dependence is what our classifiers learn.

Research

What we work on

Nuclear equation of state

Classifying the dense-matter EOS from bounce signals with neural networks, now with detector noise, several progenitors, bounce-time uncertainty and multi-detector networks.

Rotation and progenitors

Estimating core rotation, peak frequency and amplitude, and progenitor mass from the bounce and early ring-down, with entropy-based features among the tools.

Detectability

How far parameters can be recovered with current and next-generation detectors. Detector networks improve accuracy and sky coverage, and third-generation observatories extend the reach roughly tenfold.
